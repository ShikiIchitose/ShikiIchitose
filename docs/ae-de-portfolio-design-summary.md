## Why This Portfolio: From Motivation to Design Rationale

## 1. Objective

This portfolio set was designed around the question of what I can demonstrate as a candidate without professional experience in Analytics Engineering (AE) and Data Engineering (DE).

Rather than building isolated technical exercises, the goal was to reproduce a small but coherent business-domain workflow: data generated by an operational application, usage event ingestion, raw data, warehouse modeling, data quality checks, and Business Intelligence (BI) dashboard artifacts.

The chosen domain is enterprise AI tool usage governance. By handling AI tool access requests, approvals, usage activity, costs, and governance exceptions, this portfolio shows how operational data can be generated, transformed into an analyzable form, and ultimately presented as material for decision-making.

This work is not intended to replace professional experience. Instead, it is intended to make visible, through reviewable portfolio projects, the kinds of responsibility boundaries, data modeling decisions, validation concerns, documentation practices, and reviewability that are expected in practical AE/DE work.

## 2. Connection to My Research Background

In graduate school, my research involved numerical computation for modeling real physical processes on computers. In numerical computation, it is important to define governing equations, initial conditions, boundary conditions, and computational conditions so that not only the results but also the process leading to those results can be reproduced, compared, and verified.

That experience connects directly to the design of this AE/DE portfolio set. Without professional experience or real business data at hand, I used publicly available information to form assumptions about business workflows and simulated a small workflow around a simulated domain of AI tool usage governance. This includes an operational application, event ingestion, synthetic raw data, a dbt warehouse, and BI dashboard artifacts.

Just as my research modeled physical processes, this portfolio models the flow of business data: how it is generated, ingested, transformed, validated, and visualized. By making source contracts, model grain, data tests, reconciliation checks, business review signals, and BI presentation responsibilities explicit, I focused on making not only the final artifacts but also the design decisions and validation process reviewable.

This work is not a substitute for professional experience. It is a record of how I tried to understand the data flow, responsibility boundaries, verifiability, and connection to decision-making required in AE/DE work under the constraint of not yet having practical industry experience.

## 3. Motivation and Design Approach

The starting question was: how can I respond to AE/DE job requirements as someone without professional experience?

I first researched what analytics engineers do in practice. From that, I concluded that it would not be enough to demonstrate only dbt-based warehouse modeling and data tests. I also needed to show the connection between the AE layer and the surrounding stakeholders, including BI reporting for business reviewers, teams, and executives.

However, as an individual developer, I do not have access to real operational business data. Therefore, I decided to create my own synthetic data and use it in dbt and analytics APIs. The goal was not to generate random data, but to design data that could plausibly arise from an operational application: requests, approvals, usage, costs, and exceptions. I then focused on whether this data could be transformed downstream into meaningful analytical models and metrics.

Based on this approach, I built the following four portfolio projects step by step around the same underlying problem:

- `analytics-metrics-api`
- `ai-tool-access-requests`
- `go-ingestion-api`
- `access-governance-warehouse`

## 4. Implementation Sequence and Iteration

This portfolio set was not built from a fully fixed plan at the beginning. It expanded step by step as I deepened my understanding of AE/DE work.

### 1. `analytics-metrics-api`

I first built a project that handles accumulated data, Key Performance Indicator (KPI) definitions, a read-only Application Programming Interface (API), and local aggregation using Parquet and DuckDB. This project served as a step before moving into the core AE layer.

Using synthetic SaaS-like event data and job-run data, I built a structure where analytical data can be accessed through an API. The purpose was to understand the interface between backend APIs and analytical data stores. Through KPI definitions, resource-oriented HTTP design, golden-output testing, and offline-first testing, I explored how data can be provided through stable and testable contracts.

### 2. `ai-tool-access-requests`

Next, based on the experience from `analytics-metrics-api`, I implemented a Django + PostgreSQL application for AI tool access requests and approvals. This project acts as a virtual operational data source that can generate business data suitable for the AE layer.

This project handles requester / reviewer / admin responsibility boundaries, authentication and authorization, Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), form validation, and automated tests for business rules. It represents the operational application layer that could produce request and approval data for a downstream warehouse.

### 3. `access-governance-warehouse` v0.1.0

After that, I assumed the kind of data that could be generated by an application such as `ai-tool-access-requests`, designed it as synthetic raw data, and implemented the core AE layer using dbt + DuckDB + Python.

The dbt project is organized into raw sources, staging, core, intermediate, and marts layers. It is designed as a warehouse that can analyze access requests, approval status, usage activity, costs, and governance exceptions. In this phase, I focused on source contracts, model grain, dbt data tests, mart design, and a static governance report.

### 4. `access-governance-warehouse` v0.2.0 / v0.2.1

In v0.2.0, I preserved the local DuckDB path as the primary clone-and-run review path while adding a cloud warehouse execution path on BigQuery. The same dbt source contract, model tree, marts, and data tests can be executed on BigQuery.

In v0.2.1, I added Looker Studio dashboard artifacts connected to BigQuery marts. To show the connection between the AE layer and stakeholder-facing reporting, I visualized three BI artifact pages: Executive Overview, Tool Adoption and Usage, and Governance Exceptions and Review Signals.

In this design, business logic and review classifications remain in dbt marts, while Looker Studio is limited to presentation, filtering, and charting.

### 5. `go-ingestion-api`

Finally, to complement the overall portfolio set, I implemented a Go HTTP ingestion API for AI tool usage events. This project represents the boundary where data flows into the upstream side of the AE layer.

It accepts one event per HTTP request and applies Content-Type enforcement, request body size limiting, strict JSON decoding, unknown field rejection, event model validation, and user / tool reference validation. Accepted events are written to append-only JSONL raw storage.

This project does not focus on analytics or transformations themselves. Instead, it explicitly handles the ingestion boundary before data enters a warehouse. It demonstrates DE-adjacent responsibilities upstream of AE work: input contracts, validation, raw storage, and separation of responsibilities from downstream systems.

## 5. Overall Project Structure

This portfolio set is intended to reproduce a small version of the following data flow.

```text
operational application / usage events
  -> ingestion boundary
  -> raw data
  -> warehouse modeling
  -> marts
  -> static report / BI dashboard artifacts
  -> stakeholder decision-making
```

The role of each repository is as follows.

| Repository | Role | Main focus |
|---|---|---|
| `analytics-metrics-api` | Analytical data access API layer | KPI definitions, read-only API, local aggregation with Parquet + DuckDB, golden-output testing |
| `ai-tool-access-requests` | Operational application layer | Requests and approvals, permission boundaries, RBAC, business rules, Django + PostgreSQL |
| `go-ingestion-api` | Usage event ingestion layer | HTTP request contract, strict validation, append-only JSONL raw storage |
| `access-governance-warehouse` | Analytics warehouse / BI layer | dbt modeling, data tests, BigQuery, Looker Studio, marts, BI artifacts |

## 6. Design Decisions and Evidence in the Implementation

Across this portfolio set, I focused not only on implementation scope, but also on which design decisions were made and where those decisions can be inspected in the artifacts.

| Design decision | Evidence in the implementation |
|---|---|
| Validate not only outputs but also transformation processes | dbt data tests, source contracts, model grain, reconciliation checks |
| Separate Data Quality from Business Logic | Transformation failures are detected by dbt tests, while business review signals are surfaced in marts / BI |
| Avoid pushing business logic into the BI layer | Review classifications and business logic are kept in dbt marts |
| Consider upstream data generation sources | Django business application and Go ingestion API are implemented as separate layers |
| Separate raw data from downstream transformations | Append-only JSONL, Parquet raw sources, staging / core / marts structure |
| Make input contracts explicit even in small implementations | Strict JSON decoding, unknown field rejection, Content-Type enforcement |
| Improve reviewability | README files, CI, releases, evidence artifacts, dashboard documentation |
| Improve runtime reproducibility | Docker multi-stage build, committed fixtures, validation commands, CI |

## 7. Implementation Principles

### Reproducibility and Verifiability

Across the projects, I made the execution steps, inputs, outputs, tests, and Continuous Integration (CI) explicit so that a third party can review the work more easily.

In the data-oriented projects, I used deterministic synthetic data, committed golden outputs, source contracts, model grain, reconciliation checks, and dbt data tests. The goal was to make not only the final outputs but also the transformation process verifiable.

### Separating Data Quality from Business Logic

In `access-governance-warehouse`, I intentionally separated data transformation inconsistencies from business signals that should be reviewed by stakeholders.

For example, duplicate rows, referential inconsistencies, grain violations, invalid enum values, negative metrics, and aggregation mismatches are treated as transformation failures that undermine warehouse reliability and should be detected by dbt tests.

On the other hand, situations such as usage without approval, approved but inactive, or spend without usage are not necessarily data corruption. These are surfaced as business review signals in marts and BI dashboards.

This design separates the responsibilities of data quality, business logic, and stakeholder-facing presentation.

### Making Responsibilities Explicit Even at Small Scale

Each repository is intentionally small. However, even in small implementations, I tried not to blur responsibility boundaries.

The operational application handles requests, approvals, and permission boundaries. The ingestion API handles event intake and persistence to raw storage. The warehouse handles transformations, aggregations, and mart definitions. The BI dashboard artifacts handle stakeholder-facing presentation.

Instead of building one large application, I split the work into multiple small repositories so that each repository can show which practical layer it corresponds to.

## 9. Portfolio Limitations and Operational Areas Expected in Practice

This portfolio set is not a production system. It is a small reviewable project using synthetic data, and it does not cover real enterprise data, real user scale, security requirements, operational monitoring, access control, cost control, data contract management, or incident response in full.

At the same time, it is designed so that important practical concerns can be reviewed at a small scale: responsibility boundaries, verifiability, data quality, business logic, and separation of BI presentation responsibilities.

### ETL / ELT Scope Boundary

This portfolio set is closer to an ELT-oriented analytics engineering workflow than to a full production ETL ingestion platform.

In an ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) workflow, data is transformed before it is loaded into the target warehouse or analytical storage. This can be important when sensitive data must be filtered, masked, standardized, or validated before storage, or when large-scale batch or streaming processing is required before loading.

In an ELT (Extract, Load, Transform) workflow, raw or lightly processed data is loaded into the warehouse first, and transformation happens afterward inside the warehouse. My current portfolio mainly demonstrates this side: controlled raw data, dbt source contracts, staging models, reusable facts and dimensions, intermediate re-graining logic, business-facing marts, tests, documentation, and BI-ready outputs.

The portfolio does not claim to implement a complete production ETL workflow. For example, it does not cover large-scale Dataflow / Apache Beam-style processing, production streaming ingestion, enterprise-scale pre-load masking, or source-specific extraction pipelines in full.

I recognize those upstream ETL and data processing workflows as important adjacent areas of data engineering. Where actual production systems require them, I would treat them as areas to learn deliberately and contribute to based on the team's architecture, governance requirements, and operational practices.

The following are operational areas that are not fully implemented in this individual portfolio, but that I recognize as necessary in actual enterprise environments.

| Operational area expected in practice | Description |
|---|---|
| Data contract / schema governance | Agreeing on and managing schema, update frequency, missing or delayed data handling, and breaking changes between upstream systems and downstream analytical platforms |
| Access control / security | Designing permissions, authentication and authorization, least privilege, and handling of personal or confidential information when working with real data |
| Production ingestion | Considering production-grade ingestion patterns such as message queues, object storage, streaming ingestion, and batch loading, beyond local JSONL storage |
| Workflow orchestration | Scheduling data generation, ingestion, transformation, tests, and report generation while managing dependencies and retries after failures |
| Data freshness monitoring | Monitoring whether data arrives by expected times and whether specific tables or marts are stale |
| Data quality monitoring | Continuously monitoring not only dbt tests but also anomalies, sudden volume changes, distribution shifts, and increases in null values |
| CI/CD for data pipelines | Running validation for SQL, dbt models, tests, documentation, and sample builds on each code change before production deployment |
| Audit logging | Making it possible to trace who accessed which data or dashboard, when they accessed it, and which processes were executed |
| Incident response | Detecting, isolating, recovering from, and communicating issues such as pipeline failures, data delays, incorrect aggregations, or dashboard inconsistencies |
| Cost management | Managing query costs, storage costs, execution frequency, and partitioning / clustering design in cloud data warehouses such as BigQuery |
| Stakeholder alignment and operations | Incorporating metric definition changes, dashboard updates, alignment with business teams, regular reviews, and inquiry handling into ongoing operations |
| Applied analytics / ML connection | Connecting marts and metric definitions to future analysis, experiment design, feature engineering, and Machine Learning (ML) pipelines |

These areas are not fully implemented in the current portfolio. I recognize them as areas to develop further through professional experience. In this portfolio set, I focused on the foundational layer: responsibility boundaries and basic design concerns across data generation, ingestion, transformation, validation, and visualization.

## 10. Conclusion

This portfolio set started from the question of what I can demonstrate to AE/DE roles as someone without professional experience.

I applied the idea I developed through numerical computation research: modeling reality and treating it as a verifiable computational experiment. Under the constraint of not having real business data, I defined a simulated business domain around AI tool usage governance and built, step by step, an operational application, usage event ingestion, synthetic raw data, dbt warehouse modeling, a BigQuery execution path, and Looker Studio dashboard artifacts.

Through this work, I aimed to demonstrate not only individual technical skills, but also the ability to design, implement, and validate a small but coherent flow that transforms business data into decision-ready analytical outputs.
